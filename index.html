A game about being in the zone

flOw was Jenova Chen's thesis game at the University of Southern California's Interactive Media program, built with Nicholas Clark and scored by Austin Wintory. It was one of the games built for his thesis, Flow in Games: the idea that a game should keep each player in Csikszentmihalyi's flow zone, between boredom and anxiety, and that the player should be the one steering the difficulty.

So there is no menu and no difficulty setting. You are a small creature in a blue sea. Eat what's around you and you grow. Eat the red food when you're ready for something harder and you sink a level, where the water gets darker and the other creatures get bigger. Eat the blue food when it's too much and you float back up. Every player tunes the game to themselves without being asked to.

It was shown at the Experimental Gameplay Workshop at GDC 2006, and people played it a lot. "Addicting" was the word Jenova's fans used most. A year later the same idea, rebuilt by his new studio thatgamecompany, launched on the PlayStation 3. That studio went on to make Flower and Journey, Austin Wintory composed the music for Journey, and the blue sea on this page is where all that started.

Why the archived copies are empty

If you've found flOw on an archive site recently, you probably got a creature you could steer, a black background and nothing to eat. Flash isn't the problem; Ruffle handles the game well. The problem is that the game isn't only its SWF file.

On startup core.swf loads levels.xml from the folder it's in, and that file holds everything: each level's background colour, its food, its creatures and the bosses. It then streams Austin Wintory's music and sound effects, one MP3 per level and per event. Copy only the SWF and the game boots into a world with nothing in it.

All of these files are still on Jenova Chen's site. Only the page that used to embed them is gone. This page hosts copies of them side by side and plays them with Ruffle. The game files are unmodified.

Where the files come from

Every file comes from Jenova Chen's own server; the other links are mirrors and history.

The game, levels and music
jenovachen.com/flowingames/implementations/flowing/: core.swf, levels.xml and 41 MP3s. See the full list below.
The original page
jenovachen.com/flowingames/flowing.htm. Its "Play flOw online" link now redirects away; the Wayback Machine has the play page as it was in March 2006, along with the how-to-play and credits pages.
The offline version
flOw_04142006.zip (11 MB), still linked from the original page and still on USC's server: the same levels.xml and MP3s, three builds of the SWF, and Flash projectors for Windows (.exe) and classic Mac OS (.hqx, which won't run on a current Mac).
Source code
flOw_source.zip on the Wayback Machine: the ActionScript 2 classes, the .fla and levels.xml, from April 2006.
Internet Archive
archive.org/details/flash_flow has the three SWF builds from the offline zip on their own, without the levels.xml and music that sit beside them in the zip, which is why it plays empty. The online core.swf hosted here is a slightly different build.
